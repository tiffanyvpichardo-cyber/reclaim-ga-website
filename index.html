What we recover

Surplus funds are what's left over after a tax sale — and they're owed to you.

When back taxes go unpaid, a Georgia county can sell the property at a tax sale. If it sells for more than the taxes and costs owed, the difference doesn't stay with the county. That money — often called surplus, excess, or overage funds — belongs to the person who lost the property, or to their heirs. But no one receives it until a claim is filed, and many people never learn it exists.

⏳

There's a window. Georgia counties generally hold surplus funds for up to five years before turning them over to the state. The sooner a claim is started, the simpler it usually is.

Who can claim it?

If any of these describe you, it's worth checking.

  • The former owner
    You owned the property that was sold at the tax sale.
  • Heirs & family
    The owner has passed away and you're a rightful heir.
  • Others with a legal interest
    Certain lienholders or parties with a recorded interest.

Questions

Straight answers.

What are surplus (excess) funds?+
When a Georgia property is sold at a county tax sale for more than the taxes and costs owed, the leftover amount is called surplus, excess, or overage funds. By law it belongs to the former owner or their heirs — not the county — but it has to be claimed.
Is this legitimate?+
Yes. Georgia counties hold these funds and return them to the rightful parties once a proper claim is filed. We handle the search and paperwork and work with a licensed Georgia attorney to file, and you're never charged unless we actually recover money for you.
What does it cost?+
Nothing to start, and nothing at all unless funds are recovered. If we succeed, our fee is 10% of the amount recovered — the maximum allowed under Georgia law — and that includes the attorney who files your claim. No upfront or hidden fees.
Do I need a lawyer?+
Georgia counties will only accept an excess-funds claim from the claimant themselves or a licensed Georgia attorney. We work with licensed Georgia attorneys who file on your behalf, and their work is included in our 10% fee.
Can I just claim it myself?+
You can. As the rightful owner or heir, you may file a claim directly with the county at no cost. Many people ask us to handle it because confirming the surplus, proving you're entitled to it, and navigating competing claims can be slow and complex — but the choice is always yours, and we'll never suggest otherwise.
What if the owner has passed away?+
Heirs may still be entitled to the surplus. These claims involve extra steps to establish who the rightful heirs are, and it's exactly the kind of situation the attorney we work with helps sort out. Reach out and we'll walk you through it.
How long does it take?+
It depends on the county and the claim — often a few weeks to several months. If more than one party claims the same funds, a court process called interpleader can extend it. We keep you updated at each step.
